# Poultry: Newcastle Disease and Vaccination

Original summary of the MSD (Merck) Veterinary Manual chapters on Newcastle disease and broiler vaccination, the ILRI/IITA I-2 vaccine page, a Nigerian field trial of a heat-resistant vaccine, and Nigerian schedule research. Vaccine products, doses and timing must follow the vaccine label and the local vet.

## Poultry: Newcastle disease signs and losses

Farmer words: chicken dey gasp, dey cough, neck dey twist, leg or wing paralysed, green watery stool, plenty chickens dey die suddenly, egg dey drop, soft-shell eggs.

- Newcastle disease (ND) is a viral disease with no treatment. Signs overlap with other diseases, so a lab test is needed to confirm.
- Severe forms: dullness, loss of appetite, breathing difficulty, mucus in the mouth, swelling around the eyes and head, blue comb, watery greenish diarrhoea, and sudden death. Nervous forms add tremors, paralysis of legs or wings, twisted neck and circling.
- Moderate forms: gasping, coughing and rattling breath in young birds, and a temporary drop in egg production.
- Losses: up to 100% sickness and death in unvaccinated flocks with the most severe forms; about 50% death (up to 90% in young birds) with the nervous form. Vaccinated birds can look normal except for fewer eggs while still shedding virus.
- Spread: breath and droppings from sick birds, moving birds, people, equipment and litter; wild and pet birds.
- Look-alikes to rule out: avian influenza (acute cases), infectious bronchitis, infectious laryngotracheitis, infectious coryza, mycoplasmosis, fowl cholera.
- Action: isolate suspected birds, stop moving birds, restrict visitors, and notify the veterinary authority; ND is reportable.
(Source: MSD Veterinary Manual, Newcastle disease in poultry.)

## Poultry: Newcastle vaccination principles

- Live vaccines such as B1 and La Sota go in drinking water or as spray. About 85% of the flock must be immune for herd protection.
- Inactivated oil vaccines are given to breeders and layers, often after live vaccines; each bird is handled individually.
- Chicks can be vaccinated from day 1, but waiting until weeks 2 to 3 can avoid interference from antibodies passed from the hen.
- Vaccines prevent illness, not infection, and give no complete protection. Genotype VII strains have caused outbreaks in vaccinated flocks, so keep biosecurity.
- No antiviral treatment; antibiotics only help with secondary infections.
(Source: MSD Veterinary Manual.)

## Poultry: Typical broiler vaccination programme (example, not a Nigerian rule)

From the MSD Veterinary Manual example: day 1 Marek's disease (injected under the skin) and Newcastle disease (coarse spray); an optional second Newcastle and infectious bronchitis dose at days 14 to 21 (drinking water or spray); Gumboro (infectious bursal disease) intermediate-strain vaccine at days 14 to 21 in drinking water. The manual says actual programmes vary with local conditions, disease pressure and the vet's preference.
Gumboro timing differs by several days between sources: one manufacturer uses days 14 and 19 in normal areas and about days 11 and 16 in high-risk areas; a university lecture used days 8 to 12 then later boosters. So no single Gumboro day is right everywhere: follow the vaccine label and your local vet. Other ND and Gumboro dates found in older notes could not be verified.
(Sources: MSD Veterinary Manual; manufacturer and lecture schedules surfaced via search.)

## Poultry: Nigerian Newcastle vaccination research

- A 2016 Nigerian review table lists: Hitchner B1 at the hatchery on day 1 (spray); La Sota at 1 to 2 weeks in drinking water; Komarov at 7 to 10 weeks by injection; inactivated oil-emulsion vaccine at 16 to 18 weeks. A 2025 Nigerian Journal of Animal Production study recommended La Sota as the first dose followed by boosters with a mesogenic strain such as Komarov or R2B, and noted lower antibody levels in the first two weeks of life, so biosecurity matters. (Seen through search summaries, not read in full.)
- Village chickens in Kaduna State given one dose of a heat-resistant vaccine (NDV4HR) mixed in feed had protective antibody levels rise from about 9% to roughly 65 to 68% of birds. The study measured antibodies only (no challenge or death data), and the authors said repeat-dose timing still needed research. Feed delivery avoids catching every bird.
(Sources: Nwanta et al. 2006, Livestock Research for Rural Development 18(5); Nigerian Journal of Animal Production 2025; 2016 review.)

## Poultry: I-2 thermostable Newcastle vaccine for village flocks

- I-2 is a heat-tolerant live vaccine given by eye drop, usable in birds of all ages, mainly for village chickens.
- Schedule advised by ILRI/IITA: three doses at four-month intervals for full protection of layers; services often vaccinate every three to four months. Chick sellers should vaccinate young birds before sale.
- Handling: keep at about 8 C until use, carry in coolers with ice packs, use within 1 to 2 days of buying it refrigerated, use within 2 to 3 hours after mixing. Vials hold 100 to 200 doses.
- Doses are cheap (about US$0.02) and are given by trained, registered vaccinators.
(Source: ILRI / IITA PROPAS page on universal vaccination against Newcastle disease.)
