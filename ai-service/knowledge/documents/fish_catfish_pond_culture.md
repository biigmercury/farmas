# African Catfish (Clarias gariepinus): Pond Farming in Sub-Saharan Africa

Original summary of De Graaf and Janssen (1996), FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 362, "Artificial reproduction and pond rearing of the African catfish Clarias gariepinus in sub-Saharan Africa: a handbook", drawn from FAO field projects in the Central African Republic, Congo, Kenya and Nigeria. Figures are from that 1996 handbook and trial conditions; local results vary.

## Catfish: Pond preparation, liming and fertilising for fry

Farmer words: catfish pond, fry, fingerlings, how to prepare pond, lime, manure.

- Dry the pond bottom for a few days after cleaning to kill predators and help nutrients release.
- Liming (per 100 m2): quicklime 7 to 10 kg, slaked lime 7 to 13 kg, or agricultural lime 20 to 30 kg. Aim for pH 7 to 9 with enough alkalinity to keep pH stable.
- Fertilise: chicken manure about 50 kg per 100 m2 one week before stocking (Congo); or 10 to 20 kg manure per 100 m2 (Central African Republic). Chemical fertilisers such as superphosphate, urea or NPK 15:15:15 should be dissolved before spreading.
- Plankton density about 1.5 to 2 ml per 100 litres and a Secchi (clear-water) reading of 20 to 25 cm were used as targets in Congo; pond depth about 0.8 m for nursery ponds.
(Source: FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 362, chapter 5.)

## Catfish: Nursing fry and feeding fingerlings

- Stock 3-day-old larvae at 70 to 100 per m2 in protected ponds (traditional guideline 100 per m2). Higher numbers have been used (up to 250 per m2 in Nigeria; 278 per m2 in small covered Kenyan ponds, which gave about 32% survival to 0.3 g fingerlings).
- Week 1: live zooplankton is the key food. Bran (rice or wheat), sieved fine, 1 to 2 kg per 100 m2 per day.
- Formulated feed per 100 m2 per day, in two equal portions: week 1 0.50 kg, week 2 0.75, week 3 1.00, week 4 1.25, week 5 1.50.
- Harvest at 35 to 40 days when stocked at 100 per m2 to avoid cannibalism. By 4 to 5 weeks fish split into small (2 to 3 g) and larger (8 to 10 g) groups; survival drops sharply (1 to 3%) when fingerlings are left until over 15 g.
- Typical results in Congo: about 39% survival to 3 g fingerlings, about 1,250 kg per hectare standing crop.
- Remove tadpoles and aquatic insects early; birds and other predators need netting or other protection.
(Source: FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 362, chapter 5.)

## Catfish: Grow-out stocking, feed and feeding rate

Farmer words: how much feed, feeding rate, how many fish per pond, fish no dey grow.

- Stocking: 2 to 10 fingerlings per m2. After six months about 500 g fish at the low end and about 200 g at 10 per m2. Central African Republic static ponds: 10 per m2 gave about 10,000 kg per hectare (200 g average, about 50% survival) in 24 weeks. Do not go past 10 per m2; thin periodically in high-density systems.
- Feed: formulated feed is needed for intensive culture. Best growth at about 35 to 42% crude protein; digestible protein targets of 35 to 40% for fry and fingerlings, 30 to 35% for growers, 35 to 40% for broodstock. Plant oilseed cakes are preferred over scarce fishmeal; poultry vitamin premix at about 0.1% of pelleted feed is used where no fish premix is available.
- Feeding rate (as % of body weight per day at 25 C): about 6.5% for 1 g fish, 3.4% at 25 g, and 2.3% at 200 g. Feed between 21 and 33 C. Use slightly more in the first months, less in the last three months of a static pond. Recalculate every two weeks and re-sample fish every 4 to 6 weeks with a cast net.
- Feed conversion in South African trials was about 1.0 to 1.5.
(Source: FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 362, chapter 6.)

## Catfish: Water quality and overfeeding problems

Farmer words: fish dey gasp, fish dey come up for water surface for morning, water green, fish dey die for night.

- Overfeeding causes low oxygen, high ammonia and high suspended solids. Dense algae blooms and surface scum can cause oxygen shortage at night and just before dawn.
- Fixes: cut feeding and flush with fresh water. Heavily stocked ponds need about 25% daily water exchange.
- Poor water quality from accumulated waste stresses fish and can trigger disease outbreaks.
- Bird predation is a major problem in intensive farms; protect ponds.
(Source: FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 362, chapter 6.)

## Catfish: Breeding and hatchery basics

- Broodstock: males over 200 g and at least 7 months old; females ideally 300 to 800 g. Broodstock pond density 0.5 to 1 fish per m2. Ovaries stay ripe year-round only if water is above about 22 C.
- Eggs hatch in about 27 hours at 25 C and 20 hours at 30 C. Yolk-sac larvae need no feed for 3 to 4 days.
- Keep eggs and larvae in clean, oxygenated water and remove dead eggs and shells to prevent fungus. The 1996 handbook mentions malachite green for fungus control; this chemical is banned for food fish in many countries, so do not recommend it.
- One testis can fertilise the eggs of 10 to 15 females.
(Source: FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 362, chapter 4.)
