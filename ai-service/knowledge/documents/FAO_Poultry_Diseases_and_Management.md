# FAO Animal Health Manual: Poultry Diseases, Symptoms, and Management in Tropical Climates

## 1. Coccidiosis in Poultry (Broilers & Layers)
### Overview & Symptoms:
Coccidiosis is an intestinal parasitic disease caused by Eimeria protozoa. It is one of the most common and devastating diseases in tropical broiler farming.
- **Common Farmer Observations / Field Signs**:
  - Birds not eating or severe drop in feed intake ("birds no dey chop").
  - Droopiness, huddled posture near heat sources or corners, ruffled feathers, pale combs and wattles.
  - Watery droppings, bloody diarrhea, or mucus in feces.
  - Increased thirst and dehydration.
  - Wet litter around drinkers.
- **Predisposing Factors**: Wet, warm, and humid litter conditions; poor ventilation; high stocking density; uncleaned drinker spillages.

### Treatment and Immediate Actions:
1. **Immediate Medication**: Administer approved anticoccidial medications via drinking water immediately upon symptom onset. Common treatments include:
   - Amprolium (0.024% in drinking water for 5-7 days)
   - Toltrazuril (e.g., Baycox, 7 mg/kg body weight for 2 consecutive days)
   - Sulfonamides (e.g., Sulfaquinoxaline or Sulfaclozine) under veterinary guidance.
2. **Supportive Therapy**: Provide fat-soluble vitamins (Vitamin A and Vitamin K) and electrolytes in water to promote gut epithelial healing and reduce blood loss.
3. **Litter Management**: Remove and replace all wet, caked litter immediately. Keep litter moisture below 25%.

### Prevention & Biosecurity:
- Use coccidiostats in commercial starter and grower feed.
- Ensure nipple or bell drinkers do not leak.
- Practice strict "all-in, all-out" batch management with a minimum 14-day downtime between batches for pen disinfection.

## 2. Newcastle Disease (ND)
### Symptoms:
- Respiratory distress: gasping, coughing, rattling sounds (rales).
- Nervous signs: twisted neck (torticollis), tremors, paralysis of legs or wings.
- Greenish watery diarrhea, sudden high mortality (up to 90-100% in unvaccinated flocks), sharp drop in egg production with soft-shelled eggs.
### Control & Management:
- No effective curative treatment for viral infection.
- Strict vaccination protocol: NDV Hitchner B1 / LaSota (at Day 1-3, Day 14-21, Day 28, and every 6-8 weeks for layers).
- Immediate quarantine of suspected birds and veterinary notification.

## 3. Infectious Bursal Disease (Gumboro / IBD)
### Symptoms:
- Sudden onset in chicks between 3 to 6 weeks of age.
- Severe whitish/watery diarrhea, soiled vent feathers, vent picking, trembling, prostration.
- High mortality spike lasting 5 to 7 days before subsiding.
### Management:
- Administer Gumboro IBD intermediate or intermediate-plus strain vaccines at Day 10-14 and booster at Day 21.
- Provide electrolytes and kidney-flushing solutions (e.g., vinegar/citric acid solutions or herbal kidney protectors) in drinking water during outbreaks.

## 4. Heat Stress in Tropical Poultry Production
### Symptoms:
- Rapid panting with open beaks, wings held away from the body.
- Extreme drop in feed consumption, accompanied by a massive increase in water intake.
- Watery droppings caused by excess water consumption.
- Thin eggshells in layers and sudden death during hot afternoons.
### Management:
- Shift feeding times to cooler hours (early morning 5:00 AM - 8:00 AM and late evening after 6:00 PM).
- Add Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid) and electrolytes/sodium bicarbonate to drinking water.
- Ensure cool drinking water and optimize natural ventilation in open-sided housing.
