# Nile Tilapia: Pond Water Quality and Feeding

Evidence note: no single FAO document with combined pond targets was found. The figures below come from peer-reviewed trials and extension articles named in each section; they are research results, not an official standard. Ask a local fisheries extension officer to confirm.

## Tilapia: Dissolved oxygen (fish gasping, early-morning deaths)

Farmer words: tilapia dey gasp, fish dey float for top for morning, fish dey die after rain, green water.

- Oxygen is lowest at night and just before dawn (about 02:00 to 06:00) and highest in the afternoon (around 15:00). In Thai ponds, oxygen was below 1 mg/L during those hours in 68% of observations.
- A commonly used reference point: oxygen below about 5 mg/L is dangerous for fish growth and health, and ponds swing up and down over a day.
- Thermal layers can make it worse: deep water that is poor in oxygen can mix suddenly with surface water (turnover) after cold nights or heavy rain.
- What to do: reduce feeding on cloudy, hot or humid days; do not overfeed; add aeration or fresh water at night if you have it; avoid heavy fertilising with manure; check water at dawn.
(Sources: Thai pond diel studies (ISTA6); Tanzanian fertiliser and feeding trial. See SOURCES.md.)

## Tilapia: Ammonia and overfeeding

- Un-ionised ammonia (NH3) is the toxic form. SRAC Publication 463 (Durborow et al.) reports short-term deaths over a few days from about 0.6 mg/L and gill and kidney damage and slower growth from chronic exposure as low as 0.06 mg/L. A Nile tilapia fingerling study suggested keeping it below about 0.1 mg/L for good growth.
- The same article warns of ammonia problems when feed exceeds about 100 pounds per acre per day (about 112 kg per hectare per day, my own conversion) or when feed is wasted.
- Warning signs: fish at the surface, poor appetite, red or damaged gills, slow growth.
- Fix: stop or cut feed for a day or two, exchange part of the water, avoid heavy stocking.
(Sources: SRAC Publication 463; Nile tilapia ammonia toxicity study. See SOURCES.md.)

## Tilapia: Feeding rates and restricted feeding

- Restricted feeding (about 50% of what the fish would eat to satiation) gave good growth and low feed conversion. In an Egyptian earthen-pond trial stocked at 2.4 fish per m2, 50% satiation gave feed conversion of 0.82 and a yield of 6,880 kg per hectare per year.
- Fertilising ponds plus restricted feeding was more productive and economical than fertilising alone or feeding to satiation alone in small-scale Nile tilapia.
- A Tanzanian pond trial at 3 fish per m2 used a 25% protein diet at 5% and 2.5% of body weight per day; water quality stayed in an acceptable range in every treatment.
- Production limits: a Honduran study reported a limit near 3 fish per m2, a Thai study saw no water-quality decline at the same density with fertilisation. Start moderate (2 to 3 per m2) in earthen ponds with no aeration.
(Sources: Egyptian earthen-pond trial; Tanzanian fertiliser and feed trial; Thai pond study. See SOURCES.md.)
