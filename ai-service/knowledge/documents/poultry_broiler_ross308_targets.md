# Poultry: Broiler Growth and Feed Targets (Ross 308)

Original summary of Aviagen, "Ross 308 / 308 FF Broiler: Performance Objectives" (2022), as-hatched (mixed-sex) metric table. These are objectives achievable under good management and recommended nutrition. Aviagen itself says local factors (raw materials, extreme climate, economics) can keep average farm performance below them, so a tropical farm below the table is not automatically a problem; a sudden fall is.

## Poultry: Ross 308 broiler weight, feed intake and FCR by age

Farmer words: how much should my broilers weigh, how much feed per bird, target weight, FCR, broiler no dey grow.

Per bird, as-hatched (Aviagen 2022):
- Day 7: weight 213 g; daily feed 35 g; cumulative feed 166 g; FCR 0.780.
- Day 14: weight 533 g; daily feed 67 g; cumulative feed 535 g; FCR 1.005.
- Day 21: weight 1012 g; daily feed 105 g; cumulative feed 1155 g; FCR 1.142.
- Day 28: weight 1616 g; daily feed 145 g; cumulative feed 2051 g; FCR 1.269.
- Day 35: weight 2296 g; daily feed 180 g; cumulative feed 3211 g; FCR 1.399.
- Day 42: weight 2998 g; daily feed 207 g; cumulative feed 4586 g; FCR 1.531.
- Day 49: weight 3681 g; daily feed 225 g; cumulative feed 6115 g; FCR 1.663.
- Day-old chick weight is 44 g.
FCR here is cumulative feed divided by live weight, includes the chick's starting weight, and does not account for deaths. Weights are on-farm live weights, so they include feed in the gut. Values are rounded.
(Source: Aviagen Ross 308/308 FF Broiler Performance Objectives 2022.)

## Poultry: Working out flock feed from the table

Multiply the per-bird daily feed by the number of live birds. Example: 500 birds at day 28 should eat about 145 g x 500 = 72.5 kg of feed per day if they follow the Ross curve; at day 21 about 105 g x 500 = 52.5 kg; at day 14 about 67 g x 500 = 33.5 kg. If a flock eats well below the curve for its age, or its daily intake drops suddenly from one day to the next, check water first, then heat, feed quality (mould, a new feed batch) and early signs of disease such as coccidiosis, Newcastle disease or Gumboro. Compare with the farm's own earlier days, since a farm that has never reached the table may simply have a lower baseline.
(Source: Aviagen 2022 table; reasoning for the farmer based on it. Check the farm's own records.)

## Poultry: Broiler management points from Aviagen

- Brood so chicks find water and feed easily at placement, and plan the move to automatic feeders and drinkers at 4 to 5 days.
- Feed a highly digestible, balanced starter diet.
- Watch chick behaviour to keep them in their comfort zone; beware low relative humidity (under 50%), and start a minimum ventilation programme from day one.
- Check crop fill, feeding and drinking behaviour, and the 7-day live weight (213 g on the Ross curve) to judge how brooding went.
- Fast-growing broilers make a lot of heat, especially in the second half of grow-out. Keeping ambient temperature under 21 C from day 21 may improve growth rates, which is hard in the tropics, so ventilation, shade and cool water matter even more.
- Keep biosecurity and cleanliness high to limit disease challenge.
- An older Ross brooding guide (about 1999) suggested a starting temperature of 29 to 31 C at chick level in whole-house brooding, treating the numbers as ceilings because heat suppresses appetite. Use the current Ross Broiler Management Handbook for the full schedule.
(Sources: Aviagen Performance Objectives 2022; older Ross brooding guide surfaced via search.)

## Poultry: Water use in broilers (check before relying on it)

A commonly quoted rule of thumb is that broilers drink about 1.8 to 2 times their feed weight in water at 21 C, rising to 3 to 4 times above 30 C. This was not confirmed in the sources reviewed, so use it only as a guide: a sudden fall in water use, or birds crowding at drinkers, is a warning sign.
